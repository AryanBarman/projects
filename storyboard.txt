STORYBOARD GUIDE - from an SRT to a continuous 3D explainer (no code in this file)
==============================================================================
Purpose: a repeatable order of steps for turning one voice-over SRT (one "part", 20-70 seconds)
into a storyboard that can be handed to anyone (person or AI) to build. Follow the steps in
order. Do not start building before step 6 is written down.

Inputs you need
  - The part's SRT (text + start/end times). The SRT is the only timing source.
  - The look to keep: dark teal "blueprint" world, toon outlines, stickman characters
    (Jensen, Elon, general public) cut from the character sheets.
  - The existing engine/template for that file type. Reuse it. Never rebuild the engine.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 1  READ THE SRT AS BEATS, NOT AS SENTENCES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Copy the SRT into a table: line no. | start | end | text.
  2. Merge lines into BEATS. A beat is one idea the viewer must see. Typical part = 6-10 beats.
     Start a new beat when: the subject changes, a number/date/name appears, the tone flips
     ("But", "Then", "In fact"), or there is a pause of 0.4s+ between lines.
  3. For each beat write: start time, end time, one-sentence meaning, and the KEY WORDS
     (nouns, numbers, names, verbs of change: "ousted", "$119 billion", "sealed").
  4. Mark the HARD NUMBERS and NAMES. These must appear on screen exactly as spoken.
     Do not add facts the narration does not say (invented figures, extra statistics).
  5. Flag the 3-5 PEAK MOMENTS (the line the viewer will remember). These get the biggest
     asset, a camera move, and an impact effect.

  Output of step 1: a beat table.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 2  FIND THE THROUGH-LINE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Pick ONE object or world that carries the whole part, so it feels like one flight and not
  slides. Examples already used: a golden thread on the floor; a partnership chain link that
  breaks; a supplier web held by a pin then by a handshake; a folder that becomes a map that
  becomes a campus. Rules:
  - It must change state at least twice (grows, breaks, locks, turns red/gold, is sealed).
  - It must be visible in the first and the last beat.
  Output: one sentence "The through-line is ___ ; it goes from ___ to ___."

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 3  TURN EACH BEAT INTO A STATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Lay the beats out along ONE axis in the 3D world (usually moving away from the viewer, or
  sideways for a very long part). One station per beat or per 2 beats.
  For each station decide:
  - Where it sits (x, z). Keep 20-40 units between stations so the camera has room to fly.
  - Its ground: a plain slab, a map, a paper, a grid. Stations must be recognisable at a glance.
  - Its LIGHTING: day or night/dark. Switch lighting only on a story turn (good news -> bad news).
  Output: a station list with coordinates.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 4  CHOOSE THE ASSETS (show the idea, do not make a character talk about it)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  For every beat answer: "What object, built from boxes/cylinders/flat panels, SHOWS this idea?"
  Write each asset as one line:
     asset | what it is | which key word triggers it (time) | how it appears | what it does | when it goes
  Preferred asset kinds (cheap and readable):
     counters that climb to the spoken number; bars/towers that grow or crash; stacks that
     fuse; a sign/label with the exact spoken phrase; stamps (REVOKED, SEALED, BYPASSED);
     paths/rings that trace a boundary; objects that fall, slam, snap or open; panels with a
     chart that draws itself; a phone/paper/folder that unfolds; a lock, chain, cage or door.
  Rules:
  - Each asset appears ON its word (within ~0.2s), not before.
  - Use a metaphor only if it is obvious in 1 second. Add a 2-4 word label if not.
  - Max 2-3 new things on screen per second. Remove an asset once its beat is over, or fade it
    before the camera swings away so it never crops.
  - Labels are short: 1-4 words, max ~25 characters. Longer text goes in a small panel.
  - Every number the narrator says appears once as big text (counter if it can count).
  Output: an asset table per beat.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 5  DESIGN THE CAMERA (always moving, never static)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The camera is one continuous path through the stations. Write it as KEYS:
     time | camera position (x,y,z) | look-at point (x,y,z) | field of view
  Rules:
  1. A key at the start of every beat, and one at every peak moment.
  2. Move INTO and AWAY from depth, not only left-right. Mix: push-in, pull-back, arc around an
     object, crane up/down, fly-through a portal or arch.
  3. Never closer than ~9 units to a sign or label; a hero object should fill about half the frame.
  4. Keep text clear of the top-left headline bar and the bottom caption bar.
  5. Between stations use a fast fly or whip (~1-2s). Use a hard cut only when the narration
     itself cuts ("hard cut to the ledger"), and hold the old view until the cut time.
  6. Slow drift (small movement) during holds; add a tiny roll/sway so nothing is frozen.
  7. Field of view: 36-40 for close object shots, 44-50 for reveals and aerials.
  8. Do not put the camera inside any object. Check it against every box on its path.
  Output: a camera key table.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 6  CAST (use sparingly)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Characters walk between stations and point/gesture. They do not narrate.
  - Jensen / Elon only where the narration names them. Everyone else = general-public figure.
  - One or two characters per station at most. They walk in on their beat and walk out or fade.
  - Write for each: role | enters (time, from where) | action | exits.
  - Their feet must stand on the ground in front of the asset they relate to, not behind a sign.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 7  WRITE THE ONE-PAGE STORYBOARD (this is what you hand over)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Compose a single document in this order:
   A. Through-line sentence.
   B. Beat table (step 1).
   C. Station list with coordinates and lighting (step 3).
   D. Per-beat block: time range | station | assets (step 4) | camera (step 5) | cast (step 6) |
      headline bar text (3-6 words) + subtitle (<= 8 words).
   E. Peak moments list with the effect used (impact flash, slam, counter).
   F. "Not in the narration" list: anything you were tempted to add that the script does not say.
  Review checklist before building:
   [ ] every spoken number/name appears on screen exactly as spoken
   [ ] every beat has at least one asset that changes state
   [ ] camera key at every beat start, never still for more than ~1.5s
   [ ] no asset appears before its word
   [ ] only one through-line, visible first to last beat
   [ ] nothing invented beyond the script

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 8  HAND-OFF AND BUILD ORDER (so the build is cheap)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Use the existing engine file. Replace ONLY: the captions list, the camera keys, the world
     (assets) and the cast. Keep the audio loader, captions renderer, export buttons.
  2. Point the audio tag at the part's WAV in the audio folder.
  3. Run the sprite embed once (character images become embedded data, so exports are not blocked).
  4. Build in this order: world and camera first -> assets with their times -> cast -> headline bar.
  5. Check with 10-16 still frames: one per beat plus every peak moment. Combine 4 per sheet and look.
     Fix: cropped text, overlapping signs, characters hidden behind assets, objects too big/small,
     camera too close. Two rounds are normally enough.
  6. Never overwrite the original. Write a new "-v2" file.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
COMMON MISTAKES (seen in this project)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Camera too close: labels and characters crop. Start wide, then push in.
  - Everything appears at once: put each asset on its own word.
  - Text too long for its sign: shorten it; the sign shrinks the font to fit.
  - Characters used as the main storytelling: use assets first, characters second.
  - Re-describing the same idea with several assets: one clear asset per idea.
  - Adding numbers or claims the narrator did not say (for example a football-field comparison).
  - Hard cuts everywhere: keep them for the moments the narration itself cuts.
  - Skipping the checklist in step 7 and fixing the same problems in every frame later.

What this guide does NOT cover
  - Live playback, audio sync and motion smoothness: still frames cannot prove these.
    Test them by playing the file once the build is done.
